A little bit more about me...

I grew up in Northern California (Redding, to be exact) and earned my B.S. in Computer Science from Chico State. Shortly after graduating, I got married and moved myself and our two dogs to North County San Diego to be with my husband in 2024. I'm currently a Software Engineer by day and a Ceramicist by night (and early morning...and maybe on my lunch ;) ). This year, I started sewing again and more than half of my regularly worn clothes are handmade! I enjoy all things crafty and am always looking to try out a new hobby (I have my eyes on linocuts next) - you name it, I've probably tried it at least once!

I enjoy hiking, camping, and generally being outdoors. I absolutely love tide pooling, and have been so unelievably greatful to live close enough to witness so many wonderful intertidal creatures. I'm a huge fan of nudibranchs, octopi, leopard sharks, and eveything in between. My favorite place ever is the redwoods - I grew up camping there every summer and return as often as I am able. There is a feeling that those big trees and banana slugs give me that nothing else in the world can replace! I like to think that my love for these places is very much so reflected in my creative work - so if you love these things too then you are in the right place!

More about my pottery journey...

Growing up I was never not doing something crafty. I've been building and designing things as long as I can remember (anyone who knows me will agree!). But, my pottery journey didn't start until my junior year of high school. I took to it right away! Those two years of ceramics gave me such a strong foundation for understanding how to work with clay, and I frequently use the techniques and skills that I needed in order to flourish later on (shout out to Mr. Mehr). Working with clay every day provided me with so much experience both in throwing and hand building that I don't think I ever could have obtained elsewhere. My senior year (2020), I entered my largest piece (a ceramic giraffe) into the Shasta County Arts Council's Annual Juried High School Arts Competition and it took 1st in its category, which was pretty exciting to me at the time!

After that, I took a bit of a hiatus from pottery. Between a year and a half of online school thanks to COVID and a full course load when I was back in person, I didn't get my hands back on clay until my junior year of college. I took a semester of hand building with Professor Sue Whitmore while I was there. While I certainly learned a few new skills, I think my ability to create my own projects and develop my own style is what grew immensely during that semester.

After another short break while I finished my CS degree, I knew that as soon as I moved to SoCal I had to find a studio or some way to continue ceramics. I looked around online and found Oceanside Pottery Club. After a few months of settling in, I took my first class there to make sure I liked the vibe (spoiler alert: I did!) and it became my studio for the next two years. For the first time, I had full creative freedom over what I made out of clay and it was GLORIOUS! Paired with a community of wonderful and creative people, OPC gave me the space for so many big ideas to come to life!
